# Win on tabs that never lose work

The best opening for this browser is not "uses less memory". Brave, Chrome and Edge already sell that, and Brave Memory Saver on Maximum cut the owner's usage from 8.1 GB to 2.6 GB. The opening is **hibernation that never costs the user their work**. Every shipping tab discarder (Chrome Memory Saver, Edge Sleeping Tabs, Firefox Tab Unloading) treats a discarded tab as "reload from URL". Typed text, multi-step flow state and scroll position are lost. Users keep reporting this as data loss, even after adding exceptions or turning the feature off, and no vendor restores a discarded tab to its exact prior state. The loudest complaint of 2025–2026 is a separate one: **forced bloat, AI above all**. It hit Chrome (Gemini plus a silent ~4 GB model download), Edge (Copilot displacing the Sidebar and shopping tools), Firefox (the December 2025 "AI browser" turn) and Brave (crypto, Leo). Three market shifts open the door. Arc is shut down on 22 December 2026. The AI-browser category is consolidating, with Atlas dead within a year. Kagi pulled Orion off Windows and Linux, which leaves Windows without a calm, Chromium-compatible, independent browser. Ranked, the best angles are: (1) memory × "never lose your place" for heavy-tab users, (2) memory × calm/no-AI, (3) memory × old and low-RAM PCs, (4) memory × transparency as the proof mechanism, and (5) memory × built-in blocking on a Chromium-compatible engine. The recommendation is to lead with (1), speak in the tone of (2), prove it with (4), and recruit first users through (3). The spec already holds most of the machinery. Its biggest gaps are restoring form state after a discard, a stance on WebView2's own telemetry, and the tension between "calm/no-AI" positioning and the Phase 6 AI toolkit and AI-credits revenue line. A large share of the evidence comes from search-result summaries, not full-page reads. The last section flags where it is thin.

## Tab discarding still destroys work in every major browser

Google's own documentation describes the core flaw: a discarded tab keeps only its title and favicon, and reloading is "especially disruptive" for interactive multi-step flows. The only remedy Google offers is a manual "Always keep these sites active" list ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/memory-and-energy-saver-mode)). Microsoft frames discarding the same way ("those pages must be fully reloaded") and added "sleep before discarding" in Edge 105 to soften it ([Microsoft Edge blog](https://blogs.windows.com/msedgedev/2022/12/06/sleeping-tabs-edge-105-sleep-before-discarding/)). In Firefox, Bug 1769006 is titled "Firefox unloads tabs with entered text (Data loss issue) without low memory condition". The reporter points out that Firefox can recover text after a crash but not after an unload ([Bugzilla 1769006](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1769006)).

The user voice is sharpest on Edge. In the Microsoft Tech Community thread "These sleeping tabs are not helpful", users say they "lost work many times" on auto-saving sites, including an Amazon KDP draft and a long paragraph. They also report that "adding a site to the exception list does not stop the tabs from going to sleep" and "I turned my sleeping tabs off completely and it's still happening", and they ask Edge to "open the last state the page had, without reload" ([Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/discussions/edgeinsiderdiscussions/these-sleeping-tabs-are-not-helpful/3899609)). These quotes come from search extracts, because the network blocked the page. A small extension market exists only to patch the gap: "Keep Tabs Alive" disables discarding outright ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/keep-tabs-alive-—-disable/odgfnakiaaldipjbpnfklgdlffdaechj)), and "Form Memory" saves form input ([Chrome Web Store](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/form-memory/ldfmidbgmnnljhelchobglkcaofhbnko/reviews)). That market is the clearest sign of unmet demand in the whole research set.

The users who suffer most are heavy-tab users, and they don't hoard out of laziness. CMU's CHI 2021 study found that fear of losing information drives hoarding. **About a quarter of participants had crashed their computer from too many tabs**, and over half called tab clutter a serious problem ([CMU News](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2021/may/overcoming-tab-overload.html)). Heavy-tab users also report session restore failures, such as "Firefox drops 100s of tabs upon restart" ([SUMO](https://support.mozilla.org/pl/questions/1397924)) and session files too large to load ([Bugzilla 1464685](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1464685)). Browsers mostly answer with advice to close or group tabs, which these users resist. With hundreds of tabs, almost every revisit is a reload, so discarding makes the restore pain worse.

The project spec already targets the "don't unload what's in use" half of this, with trusted and page-reported protection, visible reasons and a zero-wrongly-hibernated metric. The research says the other half matters as much: when a discard does happen, **nothing the user typed or scrolled to should be lost**. Users also distrust settings that fail silently, as the Edge exception list shows. Showing per-tab protection state ("protected because: typing / audio / call") answers that distrust directly.

## Forced AI and bloat replaced RAM as the loudest anger

RAM complaints never go away. Zen users reported "insane RAM usage and fan spinning" and ~6.5 GB for 3–4 tabs in 2025 ([Mozilla Connect](https://connect.mozilla.org/t5/discussions/insane-ram-usage-and-fan-spining-on-using-zen-browser/td-p/85802)), and Vivaldi users describe a sluggish UI at 200+ tabs ([Vivaldi forum](https://forum.vivaldi.net/post/522306)). The 2025–2026 spike, though, was about unwanted features. Gemini reached all US desktop Chrome users in September 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/google-brings-gemini-in-chrome-to-us-users-unveils-agentic-browsing-capabilities-and-more/?rand=324)). Chrome was caught silently downloading a **~4 GB on-device model**, and its off toggle also disables scam detection ([HotHardware](https://hothardware.com/news/google-chrome-accused-of-secretly-installing-4gb-ai-model-raising-privacy-and-legal-concerns)). Fully disabling Chrome's AI takes chrome://flags work that The Register says only technically motivated users will manage ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/12/26/disable_ai_features_chrome/)). Microsoft retired the Edge Sidebar and folded the shopping tools into Copilot, over user petitions ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/software-apps/microsoft-edge-is-removing-its-sidebar-to-cut-clutter-while-leaving-the-ever-controversial-copilot-untouched); [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/edge/changes-to-shopping-in-edge)). On the Brave Community forum, threads such as "Please stop adding bloat" and "PLEASE ditch crypto adware crap" recur, and one commenter said they "comprise a majority of the posts here" ([Brave Community](https://community.brave.app/t/please-stop-adding-bloat/514954)).

Firefox shows both the strength and the limits of this anger. The December 2025 "modern AI browser" announcement forced Mozilla to ship an AI kill switch in Firefox 148 ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/03/firefox_ai_kill_switch/)). The year before, the Terms of Use rewrite had been called "the last straw" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/28/mozilla-responds-to-backlash-over-new-terms-saying-its-not-using-peoples-data-for-ai)). Yet by June 2026 **only ~1% of Firefox users had used the kill switch and ~3% had turned off any AI feature** ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/mozilla-firefox-ai-kill-switch-1-percent-smart-window-vpn)). The anti-AI group is loud, motivated and small. It is the right audience for an indie browser's first 100k users, but it is not a mass-market switching reason on its own. Security gives the stance a rational backbone beyond taste. OpenAI calls prompt injection "an open challenge… we expect to continue working on for years to come" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/hardening-atlas-against-prompt-injection)), and Brave calls it "a systemic challenge facing the entire category" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/25/the-glaring-security-risks-with-ai-browser-agents/)).

The second structural complaint is the death of real ad blocking on Chromium. Chrome 138 disabled MV2 for everyone with no way back, and remaining MV2 extensions leave the Web Store on 31 August 2026 ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/mv3/mv2-sunset)). Full uBlock Origin survives only on Firefox-family browsers and through Brave's own hosting ([Betanews](https://betanews.com/article/firefox-brave-ublock-origin-chrome-edge/)). WebView2 cannot restore it: extensions are opt-in, unpacked-only and have no toolbar UI ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-edge/webview2/reference/win32/icorewebview2environmentoptions6)). The built-in adblock-rust blocker in the spec is therefore the right answer, not a stopgap.

## Users love shell features, which a webview browser can build

Most of the features users name as reasons to stay or switch live in the browser shell, not the engine. That is good news for a thin shell over OS webviews. Arc users miss **Little Arc, auto-archive of idle tabs, Spaces, the Command Bar and Boosts**. Auto-archive is described as "the single feature that made tab anxiety go away" ([SupaSidebar](https://supasidebar.com/blog/switching-from-arc-browser), a vendor blog). Zen reviewers call compact mode the "killer feature" and Glance link previews "by far" their favourite ([popcar](https://popcar.bearblog.dev/thoughts-on-zen-browser/); [MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/stopped-organizing-tabs-zen-showed-what-browsers-do/)). Vivaldi calls tab tiling its most-loved feature ([Vivaldi](https://vivaldi.com/blog/how-to/tab-management-is-our-love-language/)). Edge's sleeping tabs win comparisons on time-based sleeping, and a Brave user who moved from Edge filed an issue because Brave only sleeps tabs above a memory threshold ([brave-browser #46228](https://github.com/brave/brave-browser/issues/46228)). Opera GX's RAM and CPU limiters are praised, though a hands-on test found the CPU limiter "less impressive" ([perfcore](https://perfcore.com/opera-gx-ram-and-cpu-limiter-do-they-actually-work/)), and a user saw ~4.5 GB in use with a 2 GB limit set ([Opera forums](https://forums.opera.com/user/v1n4mr/posts)).

The gaps have moved. Chrome shipped native vertical tabs in April 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/07/chrome-is-finally-getting-vertical-tabs/)), so vertical tabs alone no longer set a browser apart. The open space is the tab model around them: archive, spaces, peek and compact. **Firefox-style containers are still unmet on Chromium**, where they are said to be "difficult due to the architecture of Chromium" ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/firefox-multi-account-containers/)). One article claims Brave shipped Containers in July 2026, but only one source says so and it is unverified. Our stack can build containers through public APIs on all three platforms: WebView2 `ProfileName` profiles, which share processes and are cheaper than separate data folders ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/microsoft-edge/webview2/concepts/multi-profile-support)); `WKWebsiteDataStore` identifiers on **macOS 14+** ([WebKit blog](https://webkit.org/blog/14423/building-profiles-with-new-webkit-api/)); and `WebKitNetworkSession` on WebKitGTK 6 ([WebKitGTK](https://webkitgtk.org/reference/webkit2gtk/2.39.7/migrating-to-webkitgtk-6.0.html)). The macOS 14 requirement conflicts with the spec's macOS 13 minimum.

Bundled suites send a warning in the other direction. Vivaldi's mail, calendar and RSS, and Opera's messengers and VPN, earn praise but feed "sluggish" and "uses more RAM" perceptions ([DEV](https://dev.to/criticalmynd/vivaldi-browser-a-comprehensive-look-at-user-opinions-pros-cons-and-alternatives-56ll)). Sidebar web panels that hibernate like tabs would be the memory-consistent middle ground.

## Three market shifts open a Windows-shaped gap

**Arc's sunset strands its power users.** The Browser Company froze Arc in May 2025 ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/05/27/arc_browser_development_ends/)) and sold to Atlassian for ~$610M ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-acquires-the-browser-company)). It then announced that Arc stops working on **22 December 2026**, with data deleted within 30 days and no open-sourcing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/arc-browser-sunsets-december-22-as-the-browser-company-doubles-down-on-dia/)). Refugees mostly went to Zen, which grew to an estimated 500k users by June 2026 ([PiunikaWeb](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/06/11/zen-indie-browser-500000-users/); the figure is self-reported). They reject Dia as "not for power users who want sidebar workflows" ([SupaSidebar](https://supasidebar.com/blog/dia-browser-vs-zen)). Zen is Firefox-based and lacks Widevine, so Netflix and Spotify don't play on Windows or macOS per its FAQ as cited ([popcar](https://popcar.bearblog.dev/thoughts-on-zen-browser/)). A WebView2 shell renders like Edge, so Chromium-only sites work, and it should keep DRM (this needs verifying). The timing matters for planning: this project is still at the spec stage, so the migration window will close before anything ships. Displaced and unsettled Arc users will remain a reachable group in 2027, but they are mostly on macOS, which the owner cannot yet test by hand. They are a secondary target, not the launch audience.

**The AI-browser bubble is deflating.** OpenAI shut down Atlas on 9 August 2026, under a year after launch, and moved agentic browsing into the ChatGPT app and a Chrome extension ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/09/openai-is-shutting-down-atlas-but-its-ai-browser-ambitions-are-still-growing/)). Engadget's reading: "the browser is a feature, not the destination" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2212701/openai-browser-isnt-dead/)). Dia and Opera Neon charge ~$20/month ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/08/07/the-dia-browser-now-offers-a-20-month-subscription-plan/); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/computing/browsers/opera-neon-wants-usd20-a-month-to-replace-google-chrome-is-it-worth-it)). Incumbents bolting AI onto existing browsers are winning that race, so a newcomer should not compete on agents. Vivaldi's CEO publicly refused generative AI in the browser ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/08/28/vivaldi_capo_doubles_down_on/)), Helium grew from nothing to 20k+ GitHub stars as "Chromium without the junk" ([Trendshift](https://trendshift.io/repositories/14983); [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/software-apps/helium-might-be-the-perfect-web-browser)), and Kagi marketed Orion as "AI-proof" ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/12/orion-ai-proof-privacy-browser/)).

**Kagi's retreat leaves Windows open.** On 2 October 2026 Kagi stopped Orion development for Windows and Linux, citing the cost of porting WebKit ([How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/kagi-abandoning-orion-browser-windows-linux-but-code-going-open-source/)). Using the OS's own engine avoids that cost. Around it, **Windows 10 still ran ~28% of Windows desktops in September 2026** ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/windows-version-market-share/all/worldwide)), and Microsoft keeps updating Edge **and the WebView2 runtime** there until at least October 2028 ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/microsoft/windows-10/microsoft-edge-support-windows-10-until-2028-for-free)). Memory prices "skyrocketing" in late 2026 pushed even gamers back toward 16 GB ([FRVR](https://frvr.com/blog/news/valve-hardware-survey-places-16gb-ram-as-the-most-popular-config-again-as-memory-prices-skyrocket/)). Distribution, however, did not open. The US remedies ruling kept Chrome with Google and kept non-exclusive default-search payments legal ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/09/02/nx-s1-5478625/google-chrome-doj-antitrust-ruling)), so the spec's search-partnership model survives. Windows still has no DMA choice screen, because Edge was not designated a gatekeeper service ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/10/07/microsoft_edge_eu_gatekeeper)).

## "Never lose your place" ranks first of five angles

Each angle was scored on four things: whether the demand is evidenced, whether incumbents leave a gap, how well it fits the architecture, and whether a benchmark or demo can prove it. Battery, privacy, gaming and focus were assessed and demoted to supporting claims. Microsoft, Apple and Opera already own battery claims, and on Windows we run Edge's engine ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-au/windows/use-microsoft-edge-for-browsing-8b3b3797-60bc-46e3-ad3d-d0662e6fd902)). Privacy is crowded and "Edge runtime inside" invites scrutiny. Opera GX owns gaming with ~33M MAU ([Opera Q3 2025](https://investor.opera.com/news-releases/news-release-details/opera-reports-third-quarter-2025-results-ahead-expectations)). No demand data was found for focus.

| Rank | Angle | Why it wins | Main risks | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Memory × never lose your place** ("keep 300 tabs, pay for the ones you use, lose nothing") | The only clearly unsolved cross-browser complaint; it attacks Memory Saver where it is weakest, not where it is strong; maps directly onto §7 of the spec; filmable as a benchmark | Trust is binary: one lost form undoes the claim; exact state restore is hard, and form/scroll capture is best-effort on some sites; heavy-tab users also expect tab-model features (archive, search, spaces) | [Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/memory-and-energy-saver-mode), [Edge thread](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/discussions/edgeinsiderdiscussions/these-sleeping-tabs-are-not-helpful/3899609), [Bugzilla 1769006](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1769006), [CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2021/may/overcoming-tab-overload.html) |
| 2 | **Memory × calm / no AI, no crypto, no shopping** | Loudest 2025–26 complaint; cheap for us to deliver; no bundled model or sidebar process, so it reinforces low memory; a prompt-injection security rationale | Crowded (Vivaldi, Helium, LibreWolf); only ~1% of Firefox users acted on the kill switch; WebView2 is Microsoft's runtime and its telemetry is unresearched; conflicts with the Phase 6 AI toolkit and AI-credits revenue | [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/03/firefox_ai_kill_switch/), [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/mozilla-firefox-ai-kill-switch-1-percent-smart-window-vpn), [Brave Community](https://community.brave.app/t/please-stop-adding-bloat/514954), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/hardening-atlas-against-prompt-injection) |
| 3 | **Memory × old and low-RAM PCs** (Windows 10 holdouts, 8 GB laptops) | Where memory savings matter most to the user; a large, identifiable group; rising RAM prices; very provable with side-by-side Task Manager screenshots | WebView2-on-Win10 horizon is Oct 2028; no RAM data for non-gaming PCs; WebKitGTK performs poorly on weak Linux hardware, so "revive an old laptop" fails on Linux | [StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/windows-version-market-share/all/worldwide), [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/microsoft/windows-10/microsoft-edge-support-windows-10-until-2028-for-free), [WebKit bug 245783](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=245783) |
| 4 | **Memory × transparency** (per-tab memory, protection reasons, budget) | Underserved outside Opera GX and Chrome's buried task manager; turns distrust of silent settings into a feature; the panel is itself the demo | A proof mechanism more than a reason to switch; per-tab numbers are approximate with shared renderers and on macOS (XPC attribution); hard RAM caps misbehave even for Opera | [Opera GX Control](https://opera.com/gx/features/gx-control), [Opera forums](https://forums.opera.com/user/v1n4mr/posts), [MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/microsoft.web.webview2.core.corewebview2.memoryusagetargetlevel) |
| 5 | **Memory × built-in blocking on Chromium-compatible rendering** ("Brave's blocking without Brave's extras") | MV3 refugees are real; blocking cuts page RAM and CPU, an honest bridge claim; keeps Edge-level site compatibility and DRM, unlike Zen and Helium | Brave already owns it with full uBO hosting; no full uBO on WebView2; the YouTube arms race | [Chrome MV2 timeline](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/mv3/mv2-sunset), [Betanews](https://betanews.com/article/firefox-brave-ublock-origin-chrome-edge/), [WebView2 ext. options](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-edge/webview2/reference/win32/icorewebview2environmentoptions6) |

The ranking departs from the positioning researcher's own order, which put old hardware first. Old hardware is an *audience*; "never lose your place" is a *differentiator*. An 8 GB Windows 10 laptop owner can already turn on Brave Maximum and reach similar totals, as the owner's own 8.1→2.6 GB result shows. What no competitor can say is that hibernation is safe enough to leave on. The strongest steelman for angle 2 as the headline is that it is the emotionally hottest topic. But "no AI" describes what we left out, and the 1% kill-switch figure shows it moves few people on its own. It works best as tone, paired with a concrete benefit.

**Recommendation:** lead with angle 1, addressed to heavy-tab Windows users on 8–16 GB machines: *keep every tab, lose nothing, see exactly where your memory goes.* Use angle 2 as brand voice (no AI agents, no crypto, no shopping, no feeds). Make angle 4 the proof: the `lwb://memory` page and a public benchmark against Brave and Edge *with their savers on*, including whether each one kept the five realistic-mix tabs alive and their form contents intact. Use angle 3 as the first-user channel (Windows 10 and low-RAM communities), and angle 5 as a feature line, not a headline.

## Spec changes the findings call for

Most of §7 already matches what users are asking for. The changes below sharpen the parts the evidence says decide trust, and surface tensions with the positioning.

| Area | Finding | Suggested spec change | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| §7.5 Restore | Users lose typed text and flow state on discard; nobody restores exact state | Before any discard, capture form-field values (excluding passwords) and scroll position, and restore them after reload, beyond today's "scroll, best effort". Show a cached snapshot image of the page while the tab rehydrates. Add a bench case: half-filled form, forced discard, reactivate, check the field contents | High, Phase 2a |
| §7.3 / UI | Users distrust silently failing exception lists | Keep the zero-tolerance metric and visible reasons, and promote them in the UI: a tab-strip badge for "protected because…" and a one-click "keep awake" visible in the UI, not buried | High, Phase 2b (mostly specified) |
| Session robustness | 100s of tabs lost on restart; oversized session files | Specify incremental, crash-safe session writes and a 500-tab restore test (focused tab first, others Discarded) in §9 | High, Phase 1b/2 |
| Many-tab model | Auto-archive, tab search and vertical tabs are loved; vertical tabs alone no longer differentiate | Move tab search (including discarded tabs) earlier, and add optional auto-archive of long-idle tabs with full-text search over the archive. Consider vertical/compact sidebar tabs as an option on a familiar default (Arc's "too much to learn" lesson) | Medium, Phase 4 |
| Containers / spaces | Unmet on Chromium; buildable via public APIs | Add spaces backed by per-space profiles. Record that persistent multi-store on macOS needs 14+ against the spec's macOS 13 minimum (a DECISIONS entry) | Medium, post-Phase 4 |

An Arc importer, which Zen shipped in 1.24b ([warp2search](https://www.warp2search.net/story/zen-browser-124b-adds-arc-migration-shareable-boosts-and-firefox-159)), is low priority given the timing and the Mac-heavy user base. The existing plan for Chromium bookmark import covers most of what Windows users need.

## Where the evidence is thin

Several load-bearing claims rest on weak ground. **All of the beloved-features research came from search-result summaries**, because full pages could not be fetched. Much of it relies on vendor and SEO blogs with an interest in the outcome, notably supasidebar.com (which sells an Arc-sidebar replacement) and superchargebrowser.com. That includes the "228 Reddit threads → Zen" analysis, whose method was not seen. The Edge "sleeping tabs" quotes, the strongest user voice behind angle 1, come from search extracts of a thread the network blocked. All StatCounter figures (browser and Windows-version share) came via summaries. Some claims rest on a single source and are unverified: Brave Containers in 1.92, Chrome 150 removing the last MV2 flag, Comet's MAU and agent-traffic share, and Edge agentic actions being limited to Microsoft 365 Premium. Zen's 500k users is self-reported. Some critical technical claims were not researched at all: WebView2 runtime telemetry, Widevine in WebView2, and whether an embedder can assign WebView2 child processes to job objects or EcoQoS.

There is **no 2025–2026 survey ranking browser complaints**, no data on how many users switched over AI, ToU or MV3, no Reddit or HN vote counts, no Arc user-base or migration figures, and no RAM distribution for non-gaming PCs. The tab-hoarding studies date from 2021 (CMU) and 2023 (Aalto). Complaints that Memory Saver stops media or calls were not found, which should be read as "not verified" rather than "absent". Before any of this goes into public copy, spot-check the quoted phrases on the source pages.

## Conclusion

The research changes the project's competitive question. Asking "can we use less memory than Brave?" is mostly answered by the owner's own Brave measurement, and the honest answer on Windows is "only somewhat". The better question is "can we make hibernation safe enough that people stop disabling it?". Every incumbent treats discarding as a reload and pushes the risk onto the user through exception lists that users say don't work. A browser that guarantees in-use tabs stay alive, and that a discarded tab comes back with its text and position intact, would turn memory saving from a setting people fear into one they leave on. That claim can be proved with a benchmark, which "no AI" and "privacy" cannot.

The second implication is a strategic choice the owner should make early. The market is rewarding calm, AI-free browsers just as the spec's revenue plan leans on AI credits. Both can coexist only if the AI toolkit stays truly separate and absent by default. If the owner wants "no AI" as part of the identity, Phase 6 should be redesigned or dropped before Phase 4 messaging locks the brand in. Windows is the right first battlefield, because Orion left it, Arc is gone, Zen can't match Chromium compatibility there, and WebView2 is guaranteed on Windows 10 until 2028.
